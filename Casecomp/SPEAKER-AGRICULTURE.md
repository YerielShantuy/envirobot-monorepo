**Your zone: Field. 71% / 68% soil · 15 NTU water.**

> "**Agriculture goes to Field — 71 and 68.**

Target is 40s, so speak it at a normal clip and you land at 37. If you're rushed, drop the last sentence (Rocky's 71 → 142) and you're at 30s.

## Each argument, traced to a number

### 1. Field beats Desert outright — no trade-off required
Straight from the data, no interpretation needed:

- Hole 1: **71 vs 60 → +11 points**
- Hole 2: **68 vs 57 → +11 points**
- Water: **15 vs 33 NTU → 18 NTU cleaner**

Field wins on *every single measurement we took*. This is the cleanest comparison in the whole dataset — say it plainly, because it needs no assumptions.

**Note the +11 is identical on both holes.** That consistency is what makes it credible: the gap isn't one odd reading.

### 2. Rocky is disqualified by being too wet — not by being unusable
Rocky is **15 points wetter than Field on both holes** (86−71 = 15, 83−68 = 15). That's the largest moisture gap between any two adjacent zones in the dataset.

What that number means:
- FAO: *"At field capacity the water and air contents of the soil are ideal… at saturation no air is present and the plant will suffer."* Most crops tolerate saturation only **2–5 days**. — FAO, *Irrigation Water Management* Manual 1, Ch. 2
- Soil oxygen below **10%** inhibits growth; flooded corn loses **18% / 22% / 32%** of yield at 24 / 48 / 72 hours. — Univ. Nebraska–Lincoln, NebGuide G1904
- Meta-analysis of **115 studies / 2,419 comparisons**: waterlogging cuts yield **32.9%** on average, **53%** when it persists 15–28 days. — peer-reviewed meta-analysis

**Say it as:** "wetter is not better — past field capacity, extra water displaces the air roots need."

### 3. Urban is disqualified by being too dry
Urban is **31 points drier than Field on both holes** (71−40 = 31, 68−37 = 31). It's the driest zone we measured, so it carries the highest irrigation demand of the four — and it's also the only zone whose reading our sensor can separate from all the others with confidence, which is why it's better spent on housing.

### 4. Field's water is usable for irrigation; Rocky's is not
Turbidity is a field surrogate for suspended solids at roughly **1–2 mg/L per NTU**. Against the standard drip-clogging hazard table (**<50 mg/L minor · 50–100 moderate · >100 severe** — Bucks & Gilbert 1979 / Nakayama & Bucks 1991):

| Zone | NTU | ≈ TSS | Clogging hazard |
|---|---|---|---|
| Urban | 6 | 6–12 mg/L | minor |
| **Field** | **15** | **15–30 mg/L** | **minor** |
| Desert | 33 | 33–66 mg/L | minor → moderate |
| Rocky | 71 | 71–142 mg/L | **up to severe** |

Field stays in the minor band **even at the worst end of the conversion**. Rocky reaches severe. That's a real operational difference: severe-hazard water needs filtration and flushing regimes that minor-hazard water doesn't.

### 5. Farming Field also protects it from the alternative
Two data-independent planning facts worth one sentence if you're asked why not build houses on Field instead:

- Soil is effectively non-renewable — **>1,000 years to form 1 cm**, and **33%** of global soils are already degraded (FAO & ITPS, *Status of the World's Soil Resources*, 2015). Sealing our best farmland under housing is not reversible.
- The US Farmland Protection Policy Act forces a project scoring **≥160 points** on NRCS form AD-1006 to go back and consider sites with less farmland impact (USDA NRCS, FPPA Manual Part 523). Avoiding prime farmland is standard practice, not a preference.

## The trade-off — say it before you're asked

**Field at 71 and 68 percent is on the wet side of ideal, and we're accepting a drainage cost.**

It's our second-wettest zone. If field capacity for this soil sits below 71, the land needs subsurface drainage — GRDC costs that at roughly **$3,000–$5,200 per hectare**. We accept that because the alternative zones fail worse: Rocky is 15 points wetter still, and Desert costs us 11 points of moisture on both holes plus 18 NTU of water quality.

Second, smaller one: irrigated agriculture is a nutrient and sediment source. Field sits on 15 NTU water and we'd want that monitored, not assumed stable.

## Questions you'll get

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| **"Rocky has more water. Why not farm the wettest zone?"** | Because 86 and 83 is saturation, not abundance. Past field capacity the extra water displaces soil air — FAO says most crops fail in 2 to 5 days saturated, and the 115-study meta-analysis puts average waterlogging loss at 33%. Field at 71 and 68 is 15 points back from that on both holes, which is the productive side of the line. |
| **"Why Field over Desert? They're both mid-range."** | Field wins on every measurement we took — 11 points more moisture on hole 1, 11 points more on hole 2, and 18 NTU cleaner water. There's no trade-off to weigh; it's a clean sweep. |
| **"Where is field capacity on your scale?"** | We can't give you an absolute number and we won't pretend to — these are relative capacitive readings, not volumetric water content, so we rank zones against each other rather than against an agronomic threshold. What we can say is the ordering, and that Rocky is 15 points past Field in the direction that hurts. |
| **"Your sensor's error bars overlap Field and Desert."** | They do at the high end of the sensor's stated variability, and we concede that. What we lean on is that the 11-point gap appears on **both** holes with the same sign, and that a completely independent instrument agrees — turbidity separates them by 18 NTU. |
| **"Is 15 NTU actually fine to irrigate with?"** | At 1 to 2 milligrams per litre per NTU it's 15 to 30 mg/L of solids, which is the minor band on the standard clogging table — under 50. Even at the worst end of that conversion it stays minor. Rocky's 71 NTU reaches up to 142, which is severe. |
| **"Two holes for a whole quarter?"** | It's our biggest limitation. Two holes can't characterise a quarter, and assigning one land use to a whole zone is a stronger assumption than averaging, not a weaker one. Before any planting we'd grid the zone properly and get field capacity and drainage rate directly. |

## Do not say

- ❌ "71% is the ideal moisture for crops" — you don't know that. It's a **relative** reading, not VWC, and you have no field-capacity value for this soil.
- ❌ "Field averages 69.5%" — never average the holes. Say **71 and 68**.
- ❌ "Field has good drainage" — you measured moisture at one instant, not a drainage *rate*. Waterlogging is about how long water sits, and you never measured that.
- ❌ Any claim about soil type, salinity, nutrients or pH. You measured moisture and turbidity. Nothing else.

## Say instead

- ✅ "Field is the only zone that's extreme on neither measurement."
- ✅ "It beats Desert on all three numbers — 11, 11, and 18."
- ✅ "Rocky is 15 points past us on both holes, in the direction that kills yield."
- ✅ "We're accepting a drainage cost, roughly 3 to 5 thousand a hectare."
