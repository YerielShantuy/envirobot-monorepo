The EnviroBot Arena is a tabletop environmental testing arena built as a square measuring approximately 1500 millimetres by 1500 millimetres. The arena is enclosed by raised wooden walls made from dark-stained timber, with four straight sides meeting at right-angled corners to form the boundary. The walls appear thick and sturdy, creating a contained testing environment for a robot. The arena is shown from an elevated perspective, giving it a semi-3D appearance while still allowing the entire surface layout to be visible.

Inside the arena, the floor is divided by a horizontal and a vertical centreline into four equal quadrants, each measuring roughly 750 millimetres by 750 millimetres. Each quadrant represents a different type of terrain that a robot may encounter in environmental monitoring tasks. The top-left quadrant is Urban: a hard grey surface resembling concrete or asphalt, flat and slightly mottled. The top-right quadrant is Field: short green artificial turf with a muted colour and a uniform texture. The bottom-left quadrant is Desert: dry tan sand with visible ripple patterns resembling miniature dunes. The bottom-right quadrant is Stone: light grey gravel and cobble with a coarse, irregular surface.

Positioned within each terrain quadrant is a water sample location. These samples are represented as small recessed rectangular slots filled with blue water. The water surfaces are slightly reflective and translucent, creating the impression of shallow water containers embedded into the terrain. There are four water sample locations in total, one for each terrain type, allowing the robot to perform water quality testing across different environmental conditions. Each slot is a wheel trap — the recess is deep enough to strand a wheel, so the robot must never drive over one.

Scattered throughout the arena are multiple soil sample markers represented by flat circular discs embedded into the ground. These markers contrast clearly against the surrounding terrain. Eight soil sample points are distributed across the arena, two within each quadrant. Their locations appear intentionally spread out to encourage navigation and exploration by the robot rather than following a simple linear path, and the exact positions are not published in advance.
